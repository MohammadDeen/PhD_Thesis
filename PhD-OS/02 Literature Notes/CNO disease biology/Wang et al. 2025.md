The paper **Wang et al. 2025**, titled “_Therapeutic approaches for SAPHO syndrome from the perspective of pathogenesis: a review of the literature_,” provides a detailed analysis of treatment strategies for SAPHO syndrome based on the evolving understanding of its biological drivers.

### **Objective: What the paper set out to do**

- **Pathogenesis-Driven Treatment:** The primary goal was to review therapeutic approaches for SAPHO syndrome by linking them to specific pathogenetic mechanisms, such as **infection, immune dysregulation, and abnormal bone metabolism**.
- **Propose a Treatment Framework:** The authors aimed to categorize treatments into three logical areas according to symptomatic manifestations to help clinicians tailor interventions.
- **Clinical Guidance:** The study set out to address the challenges of managing this rare disease in the absence of standardized, validated diagnostic and treatment protocols.

### **Methods: What the paper did**

- **Literature Review:** The authors conducted a comprehensive review of existing literature, including case studies, open-label trials, and retrospective reports.
- **Integration of Single-Center Data:** They integrated clinical observations from their own center to propose specific medication recommendations and a **stratified treatment algorithm**.
- **Pathway Mapping:** The researchers synthesized data on how various drugs target the **NLRP3 inflammasome**, Th17/Treg cell imbalance, and the RANK/RANKL bone remodeling axis.

### **Results: What was reported**

- **Pathogenesis Summary:** SAPHO syndrome likely involves an **autoimmune response triggered by low-virulence pathogens** like _**Cutibacterium acnes**_, which activates the NLRP3 inflammasome and drives a pro-inflammatory cytokine cascade (IL-1\(\beta\), IL-6, TNF-\(\alpha\), and IL-17).
- **Infection Control:** Antibiotics (minocycline, azithromycin, etc.) and **tonsillectomy** were reported as effective strategies for controlling focal infections, which may otherwise act as persistent immune triggers.
- **Immune Abnormality Management:**
    - **First-line:** **NSAIDs** are the standard for initial pain relief.
    - **csDMARDs:** Methotrexate and leflunomide are used to manage disease progression, particularly for peripheral joint and nail symptoms.
    - **bDMARDs:** Targeted biologics (especially **TNF-\(\alpha\) and IL-17 inhibitors**) show significant efficacy for refractory bone and skin lesions. Interestingly, **IL-6 inhibitors** (tocilizumab) were found to have limited or even negative effects on bone marrow edema.
    - **Small Molecules:** **JAK inhibitors** (tofacitinib, baricitinib) emerged as a promising option for refractory cases involving severe skin disease (PPP) and osteitis.
- **Bone Metabolism Therapy:** **Bisphosphonates** (pamidronate, zoledronate) are central to addressing hyperostosis and ostealgia by inhibiting excessive osteoclast activity.

### **Conclusion**

The study concludes that SAPHO syndrome requires a **combined and stratified treatment approach** that addresses its multifaceted nature. The authors propose that by concurrently **eliminating focal infections, correcting immune dysregulation, and stabilizing bone metabolism**, clinicians can achieve better disease control and improved quality of life. They emphasize that treatment must remain **individualized** and tailored to the patient's specific clinical phenotype while awaiting unified international diagnostic criteria.
