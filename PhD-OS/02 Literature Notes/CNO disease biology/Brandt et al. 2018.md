The paper **Brandt et al. 2018**, titled "_CD14(+) monocytes contribute to inflammation in chronic nonbacterial osteomyelitis (CNO) through increased NLRP3 inflammasome expression_," aimed to characterize the immune cell composition of CNO bone lesions and identify the molecular mechanisms driving monocyte-led inflammation.

### **Objective: What the paper set out to do**

- **Analyze Bone Infiltrates:** The study set out to dissect the types of immune cells present in CNO bone biopsies at various stages of the disease (early vs. late) and compare them to **bacterial osteomyelitis (BOM)** and healthy bone.
- **Evaluate the Inflammasome:** Researchers aimed to examine the expression of the **NLRP3 inflammasome** components (NLRP3, ASC, and Caspase-1) and the resulting pro-inflammatory cytokine **IL-1β** specifically in monocytes.
- **Investigate Epigenetic Regulation:** The study sought to determine if site-specific **DNA methylation** patterns contribute to the dysregulation of these inflammasome genes in CNO.

### **Methods: What the paper did**

- **Bone Biopsy Analysis:** Researchers used **immunohistochemistry (IHC)** on paraffin-embedded bone samples from 4 CNO patients, 3 BOM patients, and 2 healthy controls to quantify monocytes (CD14+), neutrophils (CD15+), T cells (CD3+), B cells (CD20+), and plasma cells (CD138+).
- **Monocyte Isolation:** CD14+ monocytes were isolated from the blood of **treatment-naive CRMO patients** and healthy controls.
- **Stimulation and Molecular Assays:** Isolated monocytes were cultured and stimulated with **LPS**. The team used **qRT-PCR** to measure mRNA expression, **Western blotting** for protein levels, and cytometric bead arrays to quantify IL-1β secretion.
- **Epigenetic Mapping:** They used bioinformatics and PCR-based assays to analyze **DNA methylation** levels at specific regions of interest (ROI) within the _IL1B_, _NLRP3_, and _PYCARD_ (ASC) genes.

### **Results: What was reported**

- **Stable Monocyte Presence:** Unlike neutrophils or lymphocytes, which varied significantly between disease stages, **monocytes were a stable component** of CNO bone lesions at all stages (early, mixed, and late). This suggests they are central "drivers" rather than transient visitors.
- **Distinguishing CNO from BOM:** While immune infiltrates were generally denser in BOM, both conditions showed significant increases in all immune cell types compared to healthy bone. In CNO, "early" lesions were characterized by more neutrophils and monocytes, while "late" lesions showed more **plasma cells**.
- **Increased Inflammasome Expression:** Monocytes from CNO patients produced significantly more **IL-1β, NLRP3, and ASC** (mRNA and protein) than healthy controls, particularly after LPS stimulation. Secretion of mature IL-1β was also elevated.
- **Epigenetic "Opening":** The study found significant **DNA hypomethylation** (reduced methylation) at the _NLRP3_ and _PYCARD_ (ASC) genes in CRMO monocytes. This lack of methylation "opens" the genes, promoting their high expression. Interestingly, the _IL1B_ gene was highly active despite showing normal methylation levels at the sites investigated.

### **Conclusion**

The study concludes that **pro-inflammatory monocytes** are key contributors to CNO pathogenesis across all disease stages. Their inflammatory phenotype is driven by an overactive **NLRP3 inflammasome**, which is itself caused by **epigenetic changes** (DNA hypomethylation) that keep inflammasome genes in a high-expression state. These findings provide a strong rationale for using **IL-1 blocking** or inflammasome-inhibiting therapies in CNO.
