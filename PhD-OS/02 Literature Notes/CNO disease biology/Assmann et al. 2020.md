The paper **Assmann et al. 2020**, titled "_Genetic variants in FBLIM1 gene do not contribute to SAPHO syndrome and chronic recurrent multifocal osteomyelitis in typical patient groups_," sought to validate previously reported genetic findings regarding the role of _FBLIM1_ in the CNO/SAPHO spectrum.

### **Objective: What the paper set out to do**

- **Clinical Characterization:** The study aimed to describe the clinical features of a cohort of patients with SAPHO syndrome and CRMO/CNO.
- **Candidate Gene Validation:** Following a 2017 study that identified _FBLIM1_ mutations in two South Asian CRMO patients, these researchers set out to determine if **_FBLIM1_ variants** contribute to the disease in a larger, independent group of "typical" European patients.
- **Pathogen Evaluation:** They also intended to evaluate the isolation rate of microbes, specifically _**Propionibacterium acnes**_, from bone biopsies to assess their contribution to disease pathogenesis.

### **Methods: What the paper did**

- **Participant Cohort:** The study included **56 European patients** (47 with SAPHO syndrome and 9 with CRMO/CNO) diagnosed at German university hospitals.
- **Genetic Sequencing:** Researchers used **Sanger sequencing** to analyze the coding exons and adjacent intronic sequences of the _FBLIM1_ gene.
- **Comparative Analysis:** They compared identified variants and their frequencies against the **gnomAD database**, the largest public group of European controls.
- **Clinical Subtyping:** SAPHO patients were categorized based on the specific combinations of symptoms they exhibited (e.g., synovitis, acne, pustulosis, hyperostosis, osteitis) to identify the most common clinical patterns.

### **Results: What was reported**

- **Lack of Genetic Association:** The study **did not identify any autosomal-recessive, truncating, or rare (<0.1%) _FBLIM1_ variants**. All identified variants had frequencies comparable to the healthy European control group.
- **Comparison to Previous Study:** The researchers could not confirm the relevance of previously suggested variants (like _rs114077715_) and found no carriers of the critical compound mutations identified in the original South Asian discovery cohort.
- **Predominant Clinical Phenotype:** Palmoplantar pustular psoriasis (**PPP**) was the most common skin manifestation (68%), and **sterno-costo-clavicular hyperostosis (SCCH)** was present in the vast majority of SAPHO patients (82%).
- **Acronym Subgroups:** The most common symptom combination was **S_PHO** (Synovitis, Pustulosis, Hyperostosis, and Osteitis), occurring in 58% of patients, while only 18% displayed the full SAPHO acronym.
- **Microbial Findings:** Germs were not discovered in **61% of bone biopsies** performed. _P. acnes_ was identified in only 26% of biopsied patients, suggesting it may not be a universal driver.

### **Conclusion**

The study concluded that while _FBLIM1_ might be relevant in specific ancestral groups (such as those of South Asian origin), **it is not a disease-causing gene in the majority of European SAPHO and CRMO patients**. The authors emphasize that "typical" patient groups do not carry the proposed _FBLIM1_ mutations, indicating a continued need to search for other genetic and immunological drivers of these complex autoinflammatory conditions.
