The paper **Li et al. 2026**, titled “_A novel SLC2A10 gain-of-function variant links glycolytic macrophage polarization to chronic nonbacterial osteomyelitis_,” identifies a new genetic driver and metabolic mechanism for CNO.

### **Objective: What the paper set out to do**

- **Genetic Discovery:** The primary goal was to identify the genetic cause of **autosomal dominant CNO of the mandible** in a Chinese family.
- **Pathogenic Mechanism:** Researchers set out to elucidate how the identified variant in _**SLC2A10**_ (encoding the glucose transporter **GLUT10**) contributes to sterile bone inflammation.
- **Link Metabolism to Immunity:** The study aimed to investigate whether altered glucose transport leads to **glycolytic reprogramming** in macrophages and subsequent pathological bone remodeling.

### **Methods: What the paper did**

- **Patient and Genetic Analysis:** The team performed **whole-exome sequencing (WES)** on a Chinese pedigree and used Sanger sequencing for validation and segregation analysis.
- **Structural and Molecular Modeling:** They conducted **3D structural modeling** of the GLUT10 protein to predict the functional impact of the identified amino acid substitution.
- **Cellular Assays:** Researchers overexpressed the wild-type (WT) and variant proteins in RAW264.7 macrophages to measure **glucose uptake**, glycolytic activity (using Seahorse metabolic analysis), and pro-inflammatory (M1) polarization.
- **Bone Remodeling Assays:** They investigated the variant's effect on **osteoclast differentiation** (TRAP staining) and its paracrine inhibitory effect on osteoblast mineralization in cocure models.
- **In Vivo Validation:** The team established a **zebrafish model** by microinjecting variant mRNA to observe systemic inflammation, mandibular macrophage infiltration, and skeletal development.
- **Transcriptomics:** Global transcriptional changes in the zebrafish model were analyzed via **RNA-seq** to map affected signaling networks.

### **Results: What was reported**

- **Identification of SLC2A10:** WES identified a novel, heterozygous **gain-of-function (GOF) variant (p.Asp292Glu)** in _SLC2A10_ that perfectly cosegregated with the disease in the family.
- **Metabolic Reprogramming:** The variant GLUT10 protein exhibited **enhanced glucose transport activity**, significantly increasing glycolytic flux and the expression of rate-limiting enzymes like HK2 and PKM2.
- **Pro-inflammatory Polarization:** This glycolytic shift drove macrophages toward a **pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype** via the **NF-κB signaling pathway**, resulting in elevated production of ROS, **IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α**.
- **Pathological Bone Destruction:** The variant directly **accelerated osteoclastogenesis** (via ERK-AP-1 signaling) and significantly **suppressed osteoblast-mediated mineralization**, disrupting bone homeostasis.
- **Zebrafish Phenotype:** Variant-expressing zebrafish showed marked **macrophage infiltration in the mandible**, systemic inflammatory responses, and impaired bone mineralization, mirroring the human phenotype.
- **Pharmacological Rescue:** Interestingly, treating cells with a glycolysis inhibitor (**2-deoxy-D-glucose**) partially rescued the abnormal inflammatory and osteoclastogenic phenotype.

### **Conclusion**

The study concludes that the **_SLC2A10_ p.Asp292Glu variant** is a novel genetic cause of mandibular CNO. It establishes that **GLUT10-mediated glycolytic reprogramming** acts as a critical upstream driver of macrophage activation and osteoclast-mediated bone destruction. By linking **immunometabolism** to autoinflammatory bone disease, the findings suggest that metabolic targeting of the glycolytic pathway could serve as a promising **new therapeutic strategy** for CNO.
