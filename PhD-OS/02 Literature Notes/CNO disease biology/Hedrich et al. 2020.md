The paper **Hedrich et al. 2020**, titled “_New Insights into Adult and Paediatric Chronic Non-bacterial Osteomyelitis CNO_,” is a comprehensive review that synthesizes decades of research to provide a detailed clinical and pathophysiological synopsis of CNO and SAPHO syndrome.

### **Objective: What the paper set out to do**

- **Comprehensive Review:** The authors aimed to describe the clinical features and molecular underpinnings of the entire CNO/SAPHO spectrum, covering both pediatric and adult presentations.
- **Summarize Pathophysiology:** A primary goal was to detail the transition from historic infectious theories to the current understanding of CNO as an **autoinflammatory disease** driven by innate immune dysregulation.
- **Guide Clinical Practice:** The study set out to summarize modern imaging strategies, establish robust differential diagnoses, and propose standardized treatment protocols to improve long-term outcomes and quality of life.

### **Methods: What the paper did**

- **Literature Synthesis:** The authors reviewed findings from nearly 50 years of clinical and basic science research.
- **Comparative Analysis:** They compared the pediatric-onset disease (CNO/CRMO) with the adult-onset phenotype (SAPHO) to identify overlaps and differences in clinical presentation and therapeutic response.
- **Pathway Mapping:** They detailed the intracellular signaling failures (e.g., MAPK/ERK) and epigenetic modifications (e.g., H3S10p) identified in CNO monocytes.
- **Protocol Development:** They proposed a **"treat-to-target" protocol** (Plan A, B, and C) based on current evidence for managing patients with and without spinal involvement.

### **Results: What was reported**

- **Molecular Hallmarks:** Monocytes from CNO patients fail to produce anti-inflammatory **IL-10 and IL-19** due to defective **ERK1/2 signaling** and an "epigenetic closure" of the _IL10_ promoter. This results in unchecked production of pro-inflammatory cytokines like **IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α**.
- **Innate vs. Adaptive Factors:** While pediatric CNO is primarily associated with innate immune mechanisms, adult SAPHO syndrome appears to involve more **adaptive immune activation**, specifically responding to **IL-17A blockade**.
- **Imaging Gold Standard:** **Whole-body MRI (WB-MRI)** was identified as the gold standard for identifying subclinical lesions and monitoring treatment, effectively replacing ionizing radiation methods like bone scintigraphy in children.
- **Genetic Landscape:** The review noted that while most cases are sporadic, rare variants in genes like _**FBLIM1**_, _**FGR**_, and _**P2RX7**_ contribute to susceptibility, and monogenic forms (e.g., Majeed syndrome) provide critical pathomechanistic insights.
- **Treatment Efficacy:** **NSAIDs** are effective first-line agents for roughly 70% of patients, but relapses are frequent (50% within 2.5 years). For severe or refractory cases, **bisphosphonates (pamidronate)** and **TNF inhibitors** are the most effective second-line options.

### **Conclusion**

The study concludes that CNO/SAPHO is an autoinflammatory spectrum characterized by a profound **cytokine imbalance**. While the clinical prognosis is generally good with early and consistent treatment, the lack of licensed medications and validated biomarkers remains a challenge. The authors emphasize that **international collaboration** is essential to stratify patients based on their molecular signatures and move toward truly individualized care.
