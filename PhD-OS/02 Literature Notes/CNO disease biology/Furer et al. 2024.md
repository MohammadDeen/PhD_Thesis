The paper **Furer et al. 2024**, titled "_Current and future advances in practice: SAPHO syndrome and chronic non-bacterial osteitis (CNO)_," is a comprehensive review of the current clinical landscape and evolving research for these autoinflammatory bone disorders.

### **Objective: What the paper set out to do**

- **Synthesize Current Practice:** The authors aimed to summarize the **current diagnostic and practical treatment approaches** to SAPHO syndrome and CNO.
- **Highlight Research Advances:** A primary goal was to spotlight ongoing international research endeavors regarding the definition and validation of **diagnostic criteria, core outcome domains, and treatment protocols**.
- **Bridge Pediatric and Adult Data:** The study set out to clarify the relationship between pediatric CNO and adult SAPHO syndrome, viewing them as a clinical spectrum.

### **Methods: What the paper did**

- **Narrative Literature Review:** The authors performed an extensive review of existing literature, including epidemiological data from cohorts in Europe, China, Japan, North America, and Australia.
- **Pathophysiology Mapping:** They summarized the latest understanding of **innate immune dysregulation**, specifically the role of the **NLRP3 inflammasome** and cytokine imbalances.
- **Imaging and Diagnostic Analysis:** The review compared various imaging modalities (WB-MRI, CT, PET/CT, and bone scintigraphy) and summarized existing (though not yet prospectively validated) diagnostic criteria.
- **Treatment Update:** They detailed current therapeutic agents, including non-biologic DMARDs, targeted biologics, bisphosphonates, and newer small molecules like **JAK inhibitors**.

### **Results: What was reported**

- **Clinical Hallmarks:** SAPHO is characterized by **anterior chest wall (ACW)** involvement in adults, while pediatric CNO primarily affects the **metaphyses and epiphyses of long bones**.
- **Pathogenic Mechanisms:** The review reaffirms that both conditions are driven by an **imbalance of pro-inflammatory (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α)** and anti-inflammatory (IL-10, IL-19) cytokines, leading to excessive **RANKL-mediated osteoclast activation**.
- **Imaging Gold Standard:** **Whole-body MRI (WB-MRI)** has emerged as the leading modality for diagnosis and monitoring, as it can detect subclinical "silent" lesions without radiation.
- **Diagnostic Delays:** Despite increased awareness, diagnostic delay remains a significant issue, reported at up to **9 years** in some adult cohorts.
- **Evolving Criteria:** New **ACR/EULAR classification criteria** for childhood CNO have been developed (shared in abstract form), and the **OMERACT group** is working to establish a core set of outcomes for clinical trials.
- **Treatment Paradigms:**
    - **First-line:** NSAIDs remain the standard initial therapy.
    - **Second-line:** For refractory cases, **bisphosphonates (pamidronate)** and **TNF inhibitors** are the most supported options.
    - **Advanced options:** IL-17 and IL-23 inhibitors, as well as **JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib)**, show promise for cases involving severe skin disease or resistant osteitis.

### **Conclusion**

The authors conclude that SAPHO and CNO are rare, debilitating conditions requiring a **multidisciplinary approach** involving rheumatologists, dermatologists, radiologists, and pediatricians. While current management is largely based on expert opinion and off-label drug use, the move toward **internationally validated criteria and outcome sets** is critical to facilitating the randomized clinical trials needed to establish a formal treatment paradigm.
