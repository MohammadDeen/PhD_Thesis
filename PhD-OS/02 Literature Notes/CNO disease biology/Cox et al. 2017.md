The paper **Cox et al. 2017**, titled “_Recessive coding and regulatory mutations in FBLIM1 underlie the pathogenesis of chronic recurrent multifocal osteomyelitis (CRMO)_,” set out to identify genetic causes for non-syndromic CRMO by focusing on rare variants in consanguineous families.

### **Objective: What the paper set out to do**

- **Genetic Discovery:** The primary goal was to find causative genetic mutations in cases of CRMO where the genetic basis was unknown.
- **Candidate Identification:** Researchers aimed to use a combination of **whole-exome sequencing (WES)** and mouse model **microarray analysis** to pinpoint candidate genes involved in sterile bone inflammation.
- **Functional Validation:** The study sought to verify the functional impact of identified variants on gene expression and bone cell regulation.

### **Methods: What the paper did**

- **Patient Cohort:** They performed WES on a South Asian child with CRMO and her consanguineous parents.
- **Screening:** They sequenced the coding regions of **_FBLIM1_** in an additional cohort of **96 CRMO subjects**.
- **Cross-Species Analysis:** Researchers analyzed the gene expression profiles of bone marrow macrophages from the **_cmo_ (Pstpip2-null) mouse model** of CRMO to identify the most significantly downregulated genes.
- **Molecular Assays:** They used **luciferase reporter assays** in fluoride-treated SaOS2 (osteoblast-like) cells to test the activity of a rare regulatory intronic variant.
- **Haplotype Analysis:** They sequenced **_IL-10_ promoter SNPs** (rs1800896, rs1800871, and rs1800872) to assess potential synergistic effects with _FBLIM1_ mutations.

### **Results: What was reported**

- **Identification of FBLIM1:** WES identified a **homozygous missense mutation (R38Q)** in the filamin-binding domain of _FBLIM1_ in the index patient.
- **Mouse Model Correlation:** Microarray analysis revealed that **_Fblim1_ was the most differentially expressed gene** in the _cmo_ mouse, showing a more than **20-fold downregulation**.
- **Second Probable Case:** A second South Asian child was found to be a **compound heterozygote**, carrying a novel frameshift mutation and a rare regulatory intronic variant (**rs41310367**).
- **Enhancer Ablation:** The luciferase assays demonstrated that the region around rs41310367 acts as an **active enhancer in osteoblasts**; the mutation significantly **ablated this enhancer activity**.
- **Epistatic Interactions:** Both affected children carried **"low-expressing" _IL-10_ promoter haplotypes** (ATA), suggesting that _FBLIM1_ mutations likely contribute to CRMO pathogenesis in an **additive manner** with genetic factors that reduce _IL-10_ expression.
- **Population Enrichment:** The study found a **two-fold enrichment** of a coding SNP (_rs114077715_) in European-American CRMO patients compared to controls.

### **Conclusion**

The study concluded that **recessive mutations in _FBLIM1_** (encoding filamin-binding LIM protein 1, or **migfilin**) underlie the pathogenesis of CRMO in some patients. The authors proposed a model where FBLIM1 acts as an anti-inflammatory regulator of bone homeostasis by blocking **ERK1/2 phosphorylation** and subsequent **RANKL expression**; when FBLIM1 is absent or dysfunctional, increased RANKL production triggers excessive osteoclast activation and sterile bone inflammation.
