# Costa et al. 2026 — BM adipocytes, PD-L1 immunosuppression, and osteoclastogenesis

## One-sentence summary

This paper argues that obesity-induced expansion of bone marrow adipocytes creates a PD-L1-positive immunosuppressive myeloid niche that promotes PD-1-positive osteoclast precursor commitment, increases osteoclastogenesis, and drives obesity-related bone loss.

## Full citation

Costa SN, Chlebek C, Gray L, Caradonna P, Ryzhov S, Rosen CJ. **Expansion of bone marrow adipocytes in obese mice leads to PD-L1-driven bone marrow immunosuppression and osteoclastogenesis.** *Bone Research*. 2026;14:32. DOI: 10.1038/s41413-026-00509-5.

This paper is relevant to my work because it connects:

It is especially useful for thinking about how non-classical inflammatory or immunosuppressive pathways can influence osteoclastogenesis.

## Main question

How does obesity-induced bone marrow adipose tissue expansion alter the bone marrow immune environment, and does this promote osteoclastogenesis and bone loss through PD-1/PD-L1 signaling?

## Background

Obesity has historically been considered potentially protective for bone because increased body weight can increase mechanical loading. However, more recent evidence suggests that obesity can impair bone quality and increase fracture risk. In diet-induced obesity mouse models, trabecular bone loss has been linked to increased osteoclastogenesis and osteoclast activity.

Bone marrow adipose tissue is no longer viewed as an inert filler tissue. It is now considered a metabolically and immunologically active component of the bone marrow niche. Since osteoclasts arise from the myeloid lineage, changes in bone marrow myelopoiesis and immune checkpoint signaling may directly affect osteoclast differentiation.

## Central hypothesis

The authors hypothesize that expansion of bone marrow adipose tissue during diet-induced obesity negatively alters the bone marrow microenvironment, leading to immunosuppressive myeloid-cell accumulation and increased osteoclastogenesis.

## Experimental model

### Mouse model

- C57BL/6J mice
- Low-fat diet control group
- High-fat diet group
- High-fat diet mice were further separated into:
  - obese high-fat diet mice
  - non-obese high-fat diet mice

The authors used predetermined obesity parameters based on change in body weight and fat mass to define obese versus non-obese high-fat diet animals.

### Key experimental approaches

- High-fat diet-induced obesity model
- μCT analysis of trabecular and cortical bone
- Histomorphometry
- TRAP staining
- Serum bone turnover markers
- Bone marrow adipocyte quantification
- Gene expression analysis of bone marrow adipocytes and visceral white adipose tissue
- Flow cytometry of bone marrow myeloid and immune populations
- Mixed lymphocyte reaction to test T-cell suppression
- In vitro osteoclast differentiation assays
- Anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1 antibody blockade during osteoclast differentiation
- Bone marrow adipocyte depletion model using BMAd-Pparg knockout mice

## Core mechanism proposed by the paper

```text
High-fat diet / obesity
        ↓
Expansion of bone marrow adipocytes
        ↓
Increased BMAT-derived Mcp-1
        ↓
Accumulation of PD-L1+ myeloid cells
        ↓
Bone marrow immunosuppression + increased PD-1+ osteoclast precursors
        ↓
Enhanced osteoclast commitment, fusion, and resorptive activity
        ↓
Trabecular and cortical bone loss
