# 2. BACKGROUND

# What is SAPHO?

SAPHO stands for:

- Synovitis
- Acne
- Pustulosis
- Hyperostosis
- Osteitis



# Core Disease Concept

SAPHO is characterized by:

## Sterile inflammatory bone disease

Especially involving:

- anterior chest wall,
- sternoclavicular region,
- spine,
- sacroiliac joints.

Associated with skin disease such as:

- palmoplantar pustulosis (PPP),
- psoriasis vulgaris,
- acne,
- hidradenitis suppurativa (HS).



# Relationship Between SAPHO and CNO

The paper strongly supports the idea that:

# SAPHO and CNO exist on a disease spectrum.

CNO:

- usually pediatric onset

SAPHO:

- usually adult manifestation

This is highly relevant to your thesis framing around:

> “Pustular psoriasis and related autoinflammatory bone disorders.”



# Important Concept:

# Autoinflammatory Bone Disease

The paper frames SAPHO/CNO primarily as:

- autoinflammatory,
- innate immune-mediated,
- cytokine-driven.

==This is important because it links directly to:==

- IL-1 signaling,
- inflammasomes,
- neutrophils,
- osteoclast activation.



# 3. EPIDEMIOLOGY

# SAPHO

- Rare disease
- Estimated prevalence:
    - <1:10,000
- Female predominance
- Often diagnosed late
- Diagnostic delays up to:
    - 9 years



# CNO

Usually:

- childhood onset,
- female predominance (~2:1),
- onset around 7–12 years.


# Important Clinical Insight

Diagnostic delay leads to:

- irreversible structural damage,
- chronic pain,
- disability.



# 4. CLINICAL PRESENTATION

# SAPHO Osteoarticular Features

## Hallmark:

Anterior chest wall involvement

Especially:

- sternoclavicular joints,
-[^1] manubriosternal region,
- costosternal joints.

This is one of the most characteristic clinical clues.



# Axial Disease

Up to:

- 50% have spinal/sacroiliac involvement.

Manifestations include:

- osteitis,
- osteosclerosis,
- hyperostosis,
- sacroiliitis,
- syndesmophytes,
- paravertebral ossification.
![[clinicaal SAPHO.png]]



# Relationship to Spondyloarthritis

The review discusses the controversy:

# Is SAPHO a SpA variant?

Arguments FOR:

- axial disease,
- psoriasis overlap,
- IL-17/Th17 biology.

Arguments AGAINST:

- osteitis pattern differs,
- HLA-B27 association weak,
- different imaging phenotype,
- response to IL-1 therapies.

Very important discussion point for your thesis.



# CNO Features

Unlike adult SAPHO:

CNO commonly affects:

- metaphyses,
- long bones,
- pelvis,
- femur,
- tibia.



# Skin Manifestations

## Most common:

- PPP
- Psoriasis vulgaris

Also:

- severe acne
- HS
- pyoderma gangrenosum


# Important Observation

Skin disease often:

- precedes osteoarticular disease by years.

This is clinically important.

# 6. DIAGNOSTIC APPROACH

# Major Problem

There are:

# NO universally validated diagnostic criteria.

---

# Imaging Is Central

## Preferred Imaging:

# MRI

Especially:

- whole-body MRI for CNO.

---

# Typical Imaging Findings

## SAPHO

- osteitis,
- hyperostosis,
- sclerosis,
- anterior chest wall lesions,
- sacroiliitis,
- vertebral lesions.

---

# Bull’s Head Sign

Bone scintigraphy:

- increased uptake at sternoclavicular region.

Classic SAPHO sign.

---

# Bone Biopsy

Main purpose:

- exclude malignancy/infection.

There is:

- no pathognomonic histology.

---

# Histology

Early lesions:

- neutrophils,
- acute inflammation.

Late lesions:

- mononuclear infiltrates,
- fibrosis,
- sclerosis.

This progression resembles chronic sterile inflammatory remodeling.

# 9. CONCLUSION

## Main Conclusions

1. SAPHO/CNO are autoinflammatory bone disorders.
2. Innate immune dysregulation is central.
3. Osteoclast activation is a major pathogenic mechanism.
4. IL-1/TNF/IL-17 pathways are critical.
5. Better diagnostic criteria and trials are urgently needed.
